# Long-Term Directions

## Description
A long-term-direction suggestion identifies a capability, body of work, market, or relationship worth developing over an extended period. It is broader than the next task and still tied to a meaningful goal or a durable opportunity, which is what keeps it from being an aspiration. The horizon is its defining property: the value arrives slowly, compounds, and cannot be obtained by concentrating effort in a short window. That shape obliges the suggestion to acknowledge uncertainty, investment, and opportunity cost rather than treating the future as a place where things simply work out. The direction must also survive the possibility that conditions change, since a horizon long enough to compound is long enough for assumptions to expire. Long-term value is not a licence for vague ambition and does not remove the need to make present choices well.

The suggestion should say what current evidence supports the direction without claiming that evidence proves it, because the honest epistemic state here is usually suggestive rather than conclusive. It should name the investment and the maintenance the direction will require, and say what is not being done instead. It should identify which assumptions are most likely to change over the horizon, since those are what the review should watch. It should offer a near-term signal capable of testing whether continued pursuit remains sensible, because a direction with no leading indicator cannot be abandoned on evidence and will therefore be abandoned on mood. It should name the review date and the criteria that apply at it. It should also say which options today's choices are preserving or foreclosing, since the cheapest form of this category is often simply not closing a door.

## Why use
Use this category when the useful observation is about a horizon rather than an action. Some of the most consequential things a caller can be told are not tasks: that a capability now nice to have will be structural in two years, that a relationship needs lead time nobody is giving it, that today's convenient choice quietly forecloses a route worth keeping. None of these fit as a next step, and compressing them into one misrepresents both the timescale and the certainty.

The category earns its place by making patience accountable. Long-horizon investments fail in two symmetrical ways: abandoned too early because no signal was defined to justify continuing, or continued too long because no signal was defined to justify stopping. Requiring a leading indicator and a review date fixes both. It converts an open-ended commitment into a conditional one, which is the only honest form a long-horizon commitment can take when the assumptions behind it will certainly move.

Distinguish it from the categories nearest to it. Strategy chooses a direction that coordinates several actions now, so it implies present subordination rather than patient development. Big bets asks for a large, concentrated, partly irreversible commitment, and calling such a commitment a direction understates the decision being made. Leverage requires specific near-term consumers, whereas a long-term direction is defined by the absence of those. Preparation builds readiness for one anticipated event with a known shape. Use long-term directions when today's actions can preserve or create a future option worth reviewing later.

## Use cases
- **A capability will matter across many future decisions.** Depth may serve better than solving each instance locally. Use it when the capability has a credible connection to future demand.
- **A durable market or audience is emerging.** Early signals may justify exploring before the opportunity becomes a project. Monitor it without pretending the outcome is settled.
- **Relationships need lead time to compound.** Trust, partnerships, communities, or institutional access cannot be built inside one task. Use it when the patient investment has a specific future value and a review path.
- **Current work can preserve a strategic option.** A small choice today may keep a future route open cheaply. Recommend it when preservation is deliberate and creates no distracting commitment.
- **Incremental work is hitting a durable ceiling.** Local improvement may not create what the next stage requires. Use it when only a broader development path changes the ceiling.
- **The horizon is being ignored.** A narrow focus may neglect a future condition that already shapes present choices. Surface it when it informs today's trades without replacing them.
- **Knowledge or assets can compound.** Repeated observations, tools, or experiments may accumulate into a real advantage. Use it when the accumulation has an owner and a plausible path to use.
- **Investment should be conditional.** The direction may deserve pursuit under review rather than indefinite commitment. Name the leading indicators and the reassessment date.
- **A dependency is becoming structural.** Reliance on a platform, supplier, or standard may deepen until it cannot be changed. Raise it while substitution is still affordable.
- **A skill base is aging.** The capability that made the current work possible may be declining in relevance or availability. Start the replacement before it becomes urgent.
- **A slow-moving external trend affects the plan.** Regulation, demographics, cost curves, or infrastructure may shift the ground over years. Track it explicitly rather than reacting late.
- **A current shortcut will compound into debt.** A convenient choice may accumulate cost that only becomes visible at scale. Name the trajectory, and the point at which it must be addressed.

## When not to use
- **A concrete action is what is needed.** A horizon does not help someone who needs to know what to do today.
- **A decision must be made now.** Deferring to a review date is not an answer to an urgent choice.
- **There is no evidence, investment logic, or review signal.** Ambition with none of these is not a direction.
- **The choice coordinates several immediate actions.** Present subordination is a different commitment from patient development.
- **A large concentrated commitment is required.** A bet framed as a direction hides its exposure.
- **Specific near-term consumers exist.** Named demand now is a reuse argument, not a horizon argument.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. A coordinating present direction belongs to strategy, and a large uncertain commitment belongs to big bets. Named near-term consumers belong to leverage, and readiness for one anticipated event belongs to preparation. An urgent decision among competing commitments belongs to prioritization, and a concrete available action belongs to immediate next steps. If the direction rests on a belief nobody has checked, use investigation before proposing the horizon.
