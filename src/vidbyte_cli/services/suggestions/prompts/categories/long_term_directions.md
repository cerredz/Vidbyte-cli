# Long-Term Directions

## Description
A long-term-direction suggestion identifies a capability, body of work, market, or relationship worth developing over an extended period. It is broader than the next task but still connected to a meaningful goal or durable opportunity. The direction should acknowledge uncertainty, investment, and opportunity cost rather than treating the future as guaranteed. It should offer a near-term signal that can test whether continued pursuit is sensible. Long-term value does not excuse vague ambition or remove the need for present choices. Use this category when today’s actions can preserve or create a future option worth reviewing.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **A capability will matter across many future decisions.** The caller may benefit from building depth rather than solving each instance locally. Use this category when the capability has a credible connection to future demand or goals.
- [ ] **A durable market or audience is emerging.** Early signals may justify exploring a direction before it becomes an immediate project. Suggest it when the opportunity can be monitored without pretending the outcome is certain.
- [ ] **Relationships require lead time to compound.** Trust, partnerships, communities, or institutional access may not be built in one task. Use this category when a patient investment has a specific future value and review path.
- [ ] **The current work can preserve a strategic option.** A small choice today may keep a future route open at low cost. Recommend the direction when preservation is intentional and does not create distracting commitments.
- [ ] **Incremental work is reaching a durable ceiling.** Local improvements may not create the capability needed for the next stage. Use it when a broader development path is necessary to change that ceiling.
- [ ] **The caller needs a horizon beyond the current task.** A narrow focus may neglect a future condition that will shape current choices. Suggest a long-term direction when it can inform present tradeoffs without replacing them.
- [ ] **A body of knowledge or assets can compound.** Repeated observations, tools, or experiments may accumulate into a meaningful advantage. Use the category when the accumulation has an owner and a plausible path to use.
- [ ] **A future review can govern continued investment.** The direction may be worth pursuing conditionally rather than committing indefinitely. Recommend it when leading indicators and a reassessment date can be named.

## Things to consider
- What future outcome or condition makes the direction worth developing?
- Which capability, asset, market, or relationship would compound?
- What current evidence supports the direction without proving it?
- What investment, opportunity cost, and maintenance will it require?
- Which assumptions are most likely to change over the horizon?
- What near-term signal can test whether pursuit remains sensible?
- Which options should current choices preserve or avoid closing?
- When and by what criteria should the direction be reviewed?

## Generation requirements

Every long-term-direction suggestion must identify a durable capability, body of work, market,
relationship, or option worth developing over an extended period while preserving near-term learning.
The proposal should:

- Name the future condition or value that makes the direction worth sustained attention.
- Connect the direction to the caller's stated outcome, current evidence, values, or capabilities.
- Explain the investment, tradeoffs, opportunity cost, and uncertainty without pretending details are known.
- Preserve future choice through near-term actions that test whether the direction remains sensible.
- Identify milestones, review signals, ownership, and conditions for continuing, changing, or stopping.
- Use supplied context to ground the direction; label market, technology, adoption, or timing assumptions.
- Distinguish a durable direction from a vague aspiration, a single strategy decision, or a large bet.
- Account for capacity, relationships, trust, maintenance, and the possibility that priorities change.
- Define a near-term signal that makes the future option more or less valuable.
- Keep sustained direction as the primary mechanism; route immediate action, broad coordination, and concentrated exposure elsewhere.

## Candidate shape

Shape the candidate as a future option with a reviewable near-term path. The reader should understand
what is being developed, why it matters, what the next signal is, and how the direction can be abandoned responsibly.

- In the **summary**, name the durable direction, future value, current rationale, and investment horizon.
- In the **action sequence**, identify the near-term capability or relationship to develop, set a learning milestone, and review the signal.
- In **decision points**, choose scope, investment pace, ownership, review date, continuation criteria, and protected options.
- In **considerations**, cover uncertainty, opportunity cost, capacity, evidence, dependencies, trust, adaptability, and reversibility.
- In **dependencies**, name capabilities, partners, access, resources, or decisions needed to preserve the direction.
- In **evidence references**, cite current signals, repeated needs, or capabilities; do not cite aspiration as evidence.
- In **assumptions**, label future demand, feasibility, timing, and value with a near-term test.
- In the **completion criterion**, require a durable direction statement plus an observable next milestone and review condition.
- If the direction requires one unusually large irreversible commitment, use big bets.

## Valid suggestion directions

Use this category for durable options:

- Develop a capability that will improve several future decisions or outcomes.
- Build a long-term relationship, reputation, audience, market position, or body of work.
- Explore a future product, service, or operating model through staged capability building.
- Preserve an option whose value depends on future information or timing.
- Invest in evidence, infrastructure, learning, or trust that compounds over time.
- Define a multi-stage direction with explicit milestones and review points.
- Narrow a broad ambition into the first durable capability worth developing.
- Protect capacity for a future opportunity without committing to the full outcome.
- Revisit the direction when a specified signal changes its expected value.

## Alignment check

The candidate is aligned when it chooses something worth developing over time and connects that choice
to a near-term signal or capability. It must be more durable than a next action but less concentrated
than a single big bet. Long-term language without investment logic, evidence, or review is aspiration.

Reject or reroute candidates that:

- Need one immediate action from the current state; use immediate next steps or continuation.
- Coordinate several current actions under a broad allocation choice; use strategy.
- Require a large concentrated commitment with meaningful downside; use big bets.
- Ask only for a factual answer or skill; use investigation or learning.
- Have no review condition or plausible reason to preserve future choice.

The primary decision must be which future option deserves sustained development and what near-term evidence keeps it honest.

## When not to use
Do not use this category when the caller needs a concrete action, a factual answer, or a decision that must be made now. Avoid it when the direction is only an aspiration with no evidence, investment logic, or review signal. If the long-term choice coordinates several immediate actions, use strategy; if it requires a large concentrated commitment, use big bets.
