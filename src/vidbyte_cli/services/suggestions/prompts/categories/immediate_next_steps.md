# Immediate Next Steps

## Description
An immediate-next-steps suggestion identifies an action the caller can take right now, from the state they are actually in. It works forward from what is true, available, and unresolved rather than backward from a plan, which is what separates it from continuation. No accepted plan is assumed to exist, and none needs to, because the claim is only that this particular move is available and worth making. Where several moves are available, the suggestion compares them and picks the one with the best immediate progress or information value. The action must be small enough to begin without another round of abstraction, since a step that requires planning to start is not immediate. Its purpose is to shorten the distance between what is known now and the next useful result.

The suggestion should prefer reversible moves, because acting before the full path is designed is only safe when being wrong is cheap. It should check for the hidden prerequisite that would make the apparent next step premature, since the most common failure here is enthusiasm about a step that cannot actually begin. It should say what result determines whether to continue, redirect, or stop, so that acting early does not become acting blindly. It should avoid creating unapproved scope or irreversible commitment ahead of decisions that belong to someone else. It should name the larger planning question it is deliberately deferring, because deferral stated is a choice and deferral unstated is avoidance. Momentum is a legitimate reason to act only when the action produces evidence or progress rather than motion.

## Why use
Use this category when the caller is stuck at an abstraction boundary — they know roughly what they want, and every route to it dissolves into more planning. In that state, further design does not help, because the information required to design well does not exist yet and will only be produced by contact with the problem. One small concrete action changes the state enough to generate a better question, and that is often worth more than an hour of thought. This is the specific situation the category is for.

It also answers a practical constraint that the more architectural categories ignore: planning has a cost and a caller's attention is finite. Where a move is cheap, reversible, and informative, taking it is usually superior to reasoning about it, and the comparison is not close. The discipline the category adds is that the move must be chosen rather than grabbed — the best available action by progress or information value, with an explicit check for the prerequisite that would make it premature.

Distinguish it from the categories nearest to it. Continuation presumes an accepted plan and advances it, which is the right category whenever such a plan exists, since the next step is then already determined. Investigation gathers evidence deliberately to inform a decision, whereas an immediate next step may produce evidence as a side effect of doing something useful. Quick wins argues from cheap payoff rather than from availability, and an immediate next step may have no payoff of its own beyond moving the state. Goal clarification applies when the destination is too vague for any action to be evaluated, which is a prior condition rather than a competing option. Use immediate next steps when something useful can start now and starting is better than deciding.

## Use cases
- **Nobody knows what can begin.** The current state may already support one useful action even with the plan incomplete. Turn availability into a concrete start.
- **Several cheap actions are possible.** Choosing one reduces delay and avoids more abstract planning. Pick the move with the strongest immediate progress or information value.
- **The next action would reveal the problem's shape.** A small inspection, conversation, draft, or measurement may make later choices easier. Use it when the action is safe and its result informs what follows.
- **The caller is stuck at an abstraction boundary.** Broad goals can hide the first observable operation. Name an action that changes the state enough to produce a better question.
- **A new task has no accepted route.** The direction may be plausible while the path stays open. Identify a reversible first move without pretending the plan is settled.
- **A dependency may already be available.** Work may be delayed on an assumption that access, approval, or input is missing. Suggest checking, when confirmation would unlock work immediately.
- **The move needs no new authority.** A small action may produce progress while larger decisions wait for an owner. Use it when nothing irreversible or out of scope is created.
- **Momentum would help and is cheap.** Starting may reduce context-switching and make the task concrete. Recommend it only when the motion produces evidence or progress.
- **The context is about to be lost.** A person, an environment, or a state may be available now and gone shortly. Act while the conditions hold.
- **The work has stalled on a decision that does not block everything.** Part of the effort may be independent of the open question. Start the independent part, and name what still waits.
- **The first step is obvious but has never been said out loud.** Groups stall on steps everyone assumes someone else identified. Say it plainly, with an owner.
- **A five-minute action would replace an hour of speculation.** A quick check may settle an argument nobody can resolve by reasoning. Recommend the check rather than the discussion.

## When not to use
- **An accepted plan already determines the next step.** Rediscovering a step the plan contains wastes the plan.
- **A required condition is missing.** An action that cannot start is not immediate, however appealing it looks.
- **The step forces a consequential decision.** Acting early must not pre-empt a choice that belongs to someone else.
- **Broad alignment has to happen first.** Starting ahead of agreement creates work that will be undone.
- **The goal is unclear.** An action cannot be the best available move when nobody can say what better means.
- **A fact must be established first.** Acting on an unchecked belief converts uncertainty into rework.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. An accepted plan belongs to continuation, and a missing condition belongs to prerequisite. An undefined outcome belongs to goal clarification, and a fact that must be settled first belongs to investigation or verification. Alignment among parties belongs to coordination, and a consequential open choice belongs to alternative, strategy, or prioritization depending on its scope. If the attraction of the step is its cheap payoff rather than its availability, the honest category is quick wins.
