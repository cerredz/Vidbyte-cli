# Reframing

## Description
A reframing suggestion offers a different interpretation of the problem, one that would change what the caller does next. It works by locating an assumption underneath the repeated symptoms — about cause, ownership, value, or constraint — and proposing that the assumption is what is failing. The new frame must stay tethered to the evidence supplied, because a frame that explains everything and predicts nothing is a slogan. Its value is measured by the actions, boundaries, or trades it changes, which is a concrete test: if the same things get done afterwards, nothing was reframed. The frame should be offered as a lens to test rather than asserted as established fact. A reframe that overclaims is harder to abandon than the interpretation it replaced.

The suggestion should name exactly what is being reinterpreted — a symptom, a boundary, a definition, an assumed cause — so the change is inspectable rather than atmospheric. It should say what evidence makes the alternative plausible and what part of the existing interpretation still holds, because most reframes are partial and pretending otherwise discards knowledge. It should show that the new account explains more than the current one without explaining more than the evidence supports. It should name the observation that would support or undermine the lens, and the small question or check that could distinguish the two frames. It should also say what decision follows if the reframe turns out to be useful but unproven, since that is the common case and the one where guidance is most needed. A reframe offered without that instruction leaves the caller holding two interpretations and no way to act on either.

## Why use
Use this category when the pattern is repeated failure of reasonable responses. When competent effort keeps not working, the most likely explanation is that the problem being solved is not the problem that exists, and no amount of additional effort on the stated problem will reveal that. Only a change of interpretation will. This is the specific situation where reframing outperforms every other category, and it is recognizable: the fixes are sound, they are applied, and the symptom returns.

The category is also the cheapest intervention available when it works. It costs a conversation and can redirect months of effort, which is a return profile nothing else in the set matches. That asymmetry is exactly why it must be disciplined: the same properties make reframing attractive as a rhetorical move regardless of merit, and an undisciplined reframe substitutes a fresh vocabulary for progress. Requiring a changed action and a discriminating check is what separates the two.

Distinguish it from its neighbours. Creative exploration generates many options inside the existing frame, which is the right move when the frame is fine and the option set is thin. Cross-domain imports a specific tested mechanism from elsewhere, which is a transfer rather than a reinterpretation. Goal clarification sharpens an outcome that is merely vague, whereas reframing replaces an interpretation that is actively misleading. Alternative changes the route while keeping the outcome fixed, and a reframe usually changes what counts as the outcome. Use reframing when changing the question promises more than repeating the answer.

## Use cases
- **The same symptom keeps returning.** Repeated fixes may be failing because the problem or boundary is misidentified. Offer an interpretation that explains the recurrence and changes the intervention.
- **The problem is framed around activity rather than value.** Effort may be measured while the intended outcome goes untouched. Shift attention to the beneficiary or result when that alters the next move.
- **Ownership sits in the wrong place.** A team may be trying to control what it cannot influence, or ignoring a lever it holds. Redraw the boundary when it clarifies responsibility.
- **A constraint is treated as a fact.** A fixed audience, sequence, resource, or quality bar may be a choice nobody revisited. Challenge it when doing so opens a credible option.
- **Current labels hide a useful distinction.** One broad problem may contain different needs, failure modes, or horizons. Sharpen the frame when the distinction changes prioritization or evidence.
- **A conflict may be a measurement mismatch.** Parties can appear opposed because they optimize different definitions of success. Reframe when a shared outcome or explicit trade makes the disagreement actionable.
- **A failure may be information rather than error.** The result may have exposed a weak premise instead of poor execution. Reinterpret it when that changes what should be tested next.
- **A different question would enable a better test.** The caller may need to test a different causal claim before continuing. Use it when the new frame yields a discriminating, bounded check.
- **The unit of analysis is wrong.** A problem framed per-user may be per-session, or per-team may be per-system. Change the unit when the pattern only appears at another one.
- **Two problems are being treated as one.** A single label may be covering unrelated causes with different remedies. Split it when the split changes what each half needs.
- **The timescale is misjudged.** A situation read as an incident may be a trend, or the reverse. Reframe when the correct timescale changes the response entirely.
- **Success is defined by the absence of complaint.** A frame built on silence may be measuring tolerance rather than value. Reinterpret when a positive definition would change the work.

## When not to use
- **The problem is well-defined and needs execution.** Reinterpreting a clear problem delays it.
- **The reframe changes no action.** A new vocabulary over the same behaviour is cost without benefit.
- **It only renames the problem.** Substituting words is not substituting understanding.
- **The evidence does not support it.** An unfalsifiable frame is harder to escape than the one it replaced.
- **Many novel possibilities are what is needed.** Widening options inside a working frame is a different move.
- **The mechanism comes from another field with evidence.** A tested transfer deserves its own discipline.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. Execution belongs to continuation, and evidence gathering belongs to investigation. Widening the option space belongs to creative exploration, and importing a tested mechanism belongs to cross-domain. A merely vague outcome belongs to goal clarification, and a different route to a fixed outcome belongs to alternative. If the reframe is plausible and unproven, pair it with experiment so the two interpretations can be separated rather than debated.
