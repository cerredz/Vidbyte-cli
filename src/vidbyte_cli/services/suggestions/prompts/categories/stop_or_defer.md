# Stop or Defer

## Description
A stop-or-defer suggestion protects attention from work whose present value no longer justifies its cost. The two halves are different decisions and should not be blurred. Stopping ends a direction and accepts the loss of the option, which is why it requires a case strong enough to be stated plainly. Deferring preserves the option and names the condition, date, or evidence that would make it worth revisiting, which is only honest if that trigger is real rather than a polite way of never returning. Both require saying what more valuable work receives the released capacity, because a recommendation to stop with nothing on the other side of it is a recommendation to do less. The category exists because continuing is the default and defaults do not get examined.

The suggestion should separate sunk cost from future value explicitly, since prior investment is the most reliable reason low-value work continues and the least valid one. It should name what option value is lost by stopping, because pretending a stop is free makes it easy to reverse and hard to trust. It should say what state, rationale, artifacts, and ownership must be preserved for a responsible restart, as a deferral that destroys its own context is a stop with extra steps. It should identify who needs to know and what ambiguity the announcement must remove, because half-stopped work consumes attention indefinitely while nobody is willing to say it is over. It should name the trigger for reconsideration where one exists, and say clearly when none does. Ambiguity about whether something is stopped is more expensive than either outcome.

## Why use
Use this category when the honest assessment is that continuing costs more than it returns, and when nothing in the situation will make that judgment for itself. Work does not stop on its own. It accumulates commitment, identity, and sunk cost, all of which argue for continuation independently of value, and the people closest to it are the least able to make the call. A suggestion that says it plainly, with the evidence and the threshold visible, is doing something structurally difficult from the inside.

The category also has a second function that is easy to miss: it makes deferral respectable. Much low-value work continues because the alternatives on offer are to keep going or to admit defeat, and a conditional pause with a named restart trigger is neither. Offering that option releases capacity without forcing anyone to write off the idea, which makes the recommendation far more likely to be acted on. The condition is that the trigger must be specific, or the deferral becomes a euphemism and the work stays half-alive.

Distinguish it from its neighbours. Prioritization ranks commitments that all remain on the list, whereas this category removes one from it. Simplification reduces the means while keeping the outcome, which is the right move when the goal is still worth something. Alternative changes the route to an outcome still worth reaching. Completion closes a remaining obligation, and where the last step is small and required, finishing usually beats abandoning. Use stop or defer when the outcome itself no longer earns its cost, or when the timing makes pursuing it now a mistake.

## Use cases
- **Expected value has fallen below cost.** New evidence may show the work will not repay its time, money, or attention. Make the reason and the threshold visible.
- **A higher-value commitment is being crowded out.** Continuing may consume the scarce resource something better needs. Name the displaced work and its clearer consequence.
- **A dependency will not arrive in time.** Waiting actively may produce repeated context switching and idle effort. Defer when the state can be preserved and a real trigger determines resumption.
- **The direction no longer fits the goal.** A changed outcome, market, constraint, or owner may have made the work irrelevant. Stop when continuing serves a commitment nobody endorses.
- **A good idea has the wrong timing.** The idea may be valuable and unaffordable under current conditions. Defer when preserving the option costs less than pursuing it.
- **More effort will not resolve the core issue.** Repeated attempts may be accumulating cost without producing new evidence. Stop when no credible intervention or learning path remains.
- **The work continues only because it started.** Existing effort creates pressure to finish regardless of value. Separate the sunk cost from the value of what remains.
- **A clean pause would reduce harm.** Continuing may increase risk, confusion, or stakeholder burden while conditions are unstable. Defer when the restart state and owner can be maintained.
- **The original assumption has been disproved.** The premise the work was built on may no longer hold. Stop rather than redirecting effort towards a goal that has dissolved.
- **Maintenance exceeds use.** An existing asset or process may cost more to keep than anyone gains from it. Retire it deliberately, with notice to whoever remains.
- **Nobody owns it.** Work with no owner consumes attention diffusely and finishes never. Assign it or end it, rather than leaving it nominally alive.
- **The same decision keeps being deferred informally.** Repeated quiet postponement is a decision that nobody has taken responsibility for. Make it explicit in one direction or the other.

## When not to use
- **The work is merely difficult.** Difficulty is not evidence about value.
- **The block is temporary and the direction sound.** A short wait is not a reason to abandon.
- **The next step is small and required for completion.** Finishing is usually cheaper than unwinding.
- **A prerequisite can be obtained promptly.** An obtainable condition is not a dead end.
- **Competing commitments need ranking.** Deciding what comes first is different from deciding what leaves.
- **A smaller, safer test would settle it.** Stopping on uncertainty forfeits information that was cheap to get.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. Ranking live commitments belongs to prioritization, and a small remaining obligation belongs to completion. An obtainable missing condition belongs to prerequisite, and a safer bounded test belongs to experiment. A different route to an outcome still worth reaching belongs to alternative, and reducing the means while keeping the outcome belongs to simplification. If the value is genuinely unknown rather than low, use investigation before recommending a stop.
