# Customer Relationship and Service

## Description

A customer-relationship-and-service suggestion improves what happens after someone buys, joins, or
starts relying on an offering. It covers onboarding, support, education, account care, renewal,
recovery, and the reasons a customer recommends the business. The category treats continued value as
an active relationship rather than an automatic result of the first transaction. A strong proposal
identifies the moment when customers become confused, disappointed, inactive, or newly successful.
It connects service work to customer outcomes and to the cost of delivering support at scale. It
should make the next improvement observable through time-to-value, retention, resolution, renewal, or
advocacy behavior.

## Why use / use cases

- **New customers do not reach first value quickly.** Early confusion can make a good offer look
  disappointing. Use this lens when a specific onboarding barrier and outcome can be observed.
- **Support requests repeat without resolving the cause.** A team may be treating symptoms manually.
  Use the category when a service change could reduce recurrence while preserving care.
- **Customers disengage after an initial success.** Value may fade because habits, reminders, or
  follow-through are missing. Use it when ongoing value can be made concrete and respectful.
- **Renewal or expansion is surprising to customers.** The relationship may not make continuing value
  visible. Use this lens when a checkpoint, education step, or proof of value could help.
- **A failure damages trust.** Recovery quality can determine whether a customer stays or leaves.
  Use it when the business can improve acknowledgement, repair, and prevention.
- **Service quality varies by person or channel.** Inconsistent answers create avoidable risk and
  effort. Use the category when shared guidance or routing can improve the experience.
- **Customers could become advocates.** People may receive real value but lack a simple reason or
  moment to recommend the business. Use it when advocacy follows a genuine outcome.
- **Support cost grows faster than customers.** More demand can overwhelm a human service model.
  Use the lens when self-service, automation, or segmentation can preserve quality.

## Things to consider

- How quickly should a new customer experience the first meaningful value?
- Where do customers become confused, disappointed, inactive, or successful?
- What outcome proves onboarding or support improved rather than merely became faster?
- Which issue should be solved at the source instead of handled repeatedly?
- What service level, recovery promise, or escalation path can actually be delivered?
- How will the change affect trust, accessibility, privacy, and customer effort?
- What support cost and staffing capacity scale with the proposed relationship?
- What behavior would show healthy renewal, continued value, or an earned recommendation?

## When not to use

Do not use this category for acquiring prospects, changing the initial price, or defining the market
before purchase. Avoid engagement tactics that optimize contact volume without customer value or trust.
Use distribution and sales for the route to purchase, business model and monetization for value
capture, and product and experience for an interaction that does not depend on an ongoing relationship.
