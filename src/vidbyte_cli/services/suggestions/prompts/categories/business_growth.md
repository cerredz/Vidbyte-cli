# Business and Growth

## Description
A business and growth suggestion improves reach, sustainability, revenue, customer value, or the conditions that support them. It should connect an action to a specific growth mechanism instead of treating growth as a universal justification. The mechanism may involve acquisition, activation, retention, expansion, pricing, distribution, or operating leverage. A credible suggestion identifies whose behavior changes and how the result would be observed. Growth ideas must account for capacity, economics, and unintended effects on trust or quality. Use this category when the primary decision concerns durable organizational or commercial value.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **A valuable audience is not being reached.** The work may have a strong offer but weak discovery, distribution, or channel fit. Use this category when a concrete audience and route to access can be named.
- [ ] **Interested people are not becoming active users.** Friction in activation, onboarding, or first value may be suppressing adoption. Suggest a growth action when the behavioral step and its measure are clear.
- [ ] **Existing customers are not retained or expanded.** A product or service may have unrealized value after the first transaction. Use it when a specific retention, renewal, cross-sell, or expansion mechanism can be tested without eroding trust.
- [ ] **Revenue does not reflect delivered value.** Pricing, packaging, contract structure, or monetization may be misaligned with the value customers receive. Recommend this category when the economic hypothesis can be evaluated with customer and cost evidence.
- [ ] **Growth is creating operational strain.** Demand may be increasing faster than support, delivery, quality, or cash capacity. Use this category to find sustainable growth constraints rather than maximizing a top-line number alone.
- [ ] **A repeatable acquisition loop is emerging.** The current work may reveal a channel, referral pattern, or message that can be made more systematic. Suggest it when repeatability and unit economics are plausible.
- [ ] **A partnership could expand reach or capability.** Another organization may provide distribution, trust, supply, or complementary expertise. Use the category when mutual value, ownership, and dependency risk can be stated.
- [ ] **A growth decision needs a measurable test.** A campaign, offer, channel, or retention intervention may warrant a bounded trial. Recommend it when the test can distinguish the relevant commercial mechanism from random fluctuation.

## Things to consider
- Which growth mechanism is being changed: reach, activation, retention, expansion, pricing, or leverage?
- Whose behavior must change for value to be realized?
- What customer problem or willingness-to-pay evidence supports the idea?
- What measure captures value rather than vanity volume?
- What acquisition, service, delivery, or support cost scales with growth?
- How could the action harm trust, quality, access, or existing customers?
- What capacity must be ready before demand is increased?
- What bounded test can separate channel, offer, and execution effects?

## When not to use
Do not use this umbrella when one commercial mechanism is the primary decision and a focused category can describe it more precisely. Use customer and market for audience or demand discovery, business model and monetization for value capture, brand and positioning for meaning and comparison, distribution and sales for route to purchase, and customer relationship and service for post-purchase value. Keep this category for cross-cutting growth plans or choices that intentionally combine several mechanisms.
Do not use this category when the suggestion is about completing an internal task, clarifying an outcome, or improving a product interaction without a business-growth mechanism. Avoid it when “more users” or “more revenue” is the only stated rationale and customer value is unspecified. If the immediate issue is a limiting operational constraint, use bottleneck; if the proposal is a long-range direction without a near-term growth mechanism, use long-term directions or strategy.
