# Adjacent Opportunity

## Description
An adjacent opportunity is a nearby source of value made more accessible by work already underway. It is not required to complete the original objective, but the current effort creates a temporary or unusually cheap path toward it. The suggestion should explain the enabling connection instead of treating mere proximity as value. It should preserve the primary commitment and define a firm boundary around any additional work. A reversible capture is preferable when the opportunity can be preserved without immediate expansion. Use this category when the incremental effort is bounded and the timing or access advantage is credible.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **Current work exposes useful information.** The task may reveal data, contacts, patterns, or decisions that support a nearby objective. Suggest the adjacent opportunity when capturing that information now is materially easier than rediscovering it later.
- [ ] **An existing setup can serve one more purpose.** A tool, workflow, interview, prototype, or dataset may support a related use with little extra effort. The suggestion should name the extra step and keep it separate from the original deliverable.
- [ ] **A short timing window is open.** A launch, meeting, market event, or stakeholder attention may make a neighboring action unusually timely. Use the category when waiting would plausibly remove the advantage rather than merely reduce convenience.
- [ ] **A reversible capture preserves future choice.** A note, export, measurement, reservation, or small spike can retain an option without committing to the full adjacent project. Recommend it when the capture has bounded cost and a clear stopping point.
- [ ] **The adjacent value compounds the main effort.** A small extension may make the current artifact reusable, discoverable, or easier to validate elsewhere. Use this category when the extra value comes from the same work rather than from starting an unrelated initiative.
- [ ] **A neighboring user or team can benefit.** The current work may solve a closely related problem for another audience with a modest adaptation. Suggest it only when the relationship is concrete and the adaptation does not silently become a second project.
- [ ] **The opportunity protects against avoidable rework.** Capturing a related decision or interface now may prevent a likely duplicate pass later. Use the category when the prevention is specific enough to justify the incremental effort.
- [ ] **The next decision depends on a nearby observation.** A quick adjacent check may reveal whether a larger follow-on path is worth pursuing. Recommend it when the check can be completed independently and its result would change a future decision.

## Things to consider
- What exact piece of current work enables the adjacent opportunity?
- Is the proposed value genuinely nearby, or is it a separate objective sharing only a topic?
- What access, attention, information, or timing advantage exists now?
- What is the smallest capture that preserves the opportunity?
- What incremental effort is required beyond the primary commitment?
- What explicit stopping point prevents scope from expanding?
- Which evidence shows the opportunity has value rather than novelty?
- What is lost if the opportunity is deferred, and how reversible is that loss?

## Generation requirements

Every adjacent-opportunity suggestion must identify a temporary advantage created by work already
underway and preserve the original commitment as the primary obligation. The proposal should:

- Name the current task, artifact, relationship, access, attention, or timing window that enables the
  neighboring opportunity.
- Explain why the connection is genuinely nearby and why doing the small capture now is cheaper or
  more reliable than rediscovering the opportunity later.
- Separate what is required for the original goal from what is optional incremental value.
- Bound the extra effort, ownership, resources, and stopping point so the suggestion cannot silently
  become a second project.
- State what evidence shows the neighboring value is useful rather than merely novel or convenient.
- Prefer a reversible note, export, reservation, measurement, or small adaptation when the full
  opportunity is not yet justified.
- Name what timing, access, information, or attention advantage would be lost by deferring it.
- Use supplied context as evidence and label the expected future value as an assumption.
- Define the observation that would justify pursuing the adjacent opportunity later.
- Keep the primary mechanism bounded capture from current work; route required work, broad strategy,
  or major expansion to the appropriate category.

## Candidate shape

Shape the candidate as an optional extension with a clear relationship to the main commitment. The
candidate should make it easy to see why this is adjacent rather than merely interesting.

- In the **summary**, name the current effort, enabling connection, nearby value, and reason timing
  makes the opportunity worth considering now.
- In the **action sequence**, complete or protect the primary work, capture the smallest adjacent
  artifact or observation, and stop at the stated boundary.
- In **decision points**, choose whether to capture, reserve, defer, or reject the opportunity and
  identify the effort threshold that controls that choice.
- In **considerations**, cover incremental cost, distraction, reversibility, timing, ownership,
  maintenance, evidence, and effects on the original deliverable.
- In **dependencies**, name the current artifact, access, contact, setup, or decision that makes the
  adjacent step cheap enough to consider.
- In **evidence references**, cite the current work or concrete timing signal that enables the
  opportunity; do not cite proximity alone.
- In **assumptions**, label expected future value, audience interest, or persistence of the advantage
  and pair it with a small capture or later review condition.
- In the **completion criterion**, require the original work to remain complete and the adjacent
  value to be captured, reserved, or explicitly deferred.
- If the extension would require a separate roadmap, team, or sustained operating model, propose a
  boundary decision rather than calling it adjacent.

## Valid suggestion directions

Use this category for optional moves that exploit the current effort without displacing it:

- Export or preserve information that would otherwise be expensive to recreate.
- Capture a reusable template, decision record, contact, measurement, or interface while it is fresh.
- Reserve a future option through a small reversible commitment.
- Add one nearby audience, use case, or consumer when the adaptation is bounded.
- Record an observation that can decide whether a later opportunity deserves investment.
- Reuse a current setup for a related purpose with an explicit stop point.
- Protect a short timing window around a launch, meeting, event, or stakeholder attention.
- Prevent likely rework by preserving a decision or compatibility seam now.
- Package a current artifact so another credible downstream user can reuse it.
- Separate a valuable capture from an attractive but open-ended expansion.

## Alignment check

The candidate is aligned when current work creates a specific, temporary, or unusually cheap path to
nearby value, and the proposed action preserves the original commitment. The idea should remain useful
even if the larger opportunity is never pursued. Convenience alone is not an enabling connection.

Reject or reroute candidates that:

- Are required to finish the stated goal; use completion, continuation, or prerequisite.
- Change the overall direction or demand a large new investment; use strategy or big bets.
- Merely improve the current result without a distinct adjacent consumer or timing advantage; use
  product experience, simplification, or another fitting category.
- Depend on an unverified market or audience with no capture that can test it; use customer and
  market.
- Create a broad commercial plan rather than a bounded extension of current work; use business and
  growth.

The primary decision must be whether to preserve or test nearby value without allowing it to displace
the original obligation.

## When not to use
Do not use this category when the suggestion is required for the stated goal, because it belongs under continuation or prerequisite. Avoid it when the connection to current work is speculative, the extra effort is open-ended, or the timing advantage is only a feeling. If the proposal changes the main direction or demands a major investment, use strategy, big bets, or another category that names that larger decision.
