# Delegation

## Description
A delegation suggestion moves a defined piece of work to the person or agent best placed to carry it. Its first act is a separation: judgment that must stay with the caller on one side, and execution, preparation, or information gathering that can move on the other. Work that has not been separated cleanly cannot be delegated, only shared, and shared work tends to return. The handoff must name the recipient, the expected result, the boundaries, the authority granted, and the path by which progress comes back. Delegation transfers execution responsibility while leaving the caller accountable for the outcome, and conflating those two is what makes delegation feel like abandonment to whoever receives it. Decisions that define quality, risk, or direction stay with the caller unless that authority is transferred on purpose.

The suggestion must price the handoff honestly, because delegation is not free and is frequently proposed as though it were. Explaining, granting access, reviewing, and correcting all cost the caller's attention, and for small one-off tasks that cost exceeds simply doing the work. The relevant comparison is between the attention released and the attention spent, over the number of times the work will recur. The suggestion should name what the recipient needs in order to start, since a handoff missing its inputs converts into a stream of interruptions. It should define acceptance evidence that lets the caller review efficiently rather than re-perform the work. It should set a check-in cadence that observes progress without reclaiming execution, because the fastest way to un-delegate something is to supervise it continuously.

## Why use
Use this category when the caller's attention is the scarce resource and some of what is consuming it does not require the caller. This is the common shape of a person who has become a constraint on their own work: the decisions only they can make are queued behind execution anyone could do. The suggestion releases the scarce input, which is a larger effect than improving any individual task, and it is frequently invisible to the person inside the situation.

The category also forces a specification that has value beyond the handoff itself. Writing down the expected result, the boundaries, and the acceptance evidence usually reveals that expectations were implicit, and implicit expectations are the actual reason work gets reviewed repeatedly. Once specified, the work can be delegated, automated, or repeated by the caller more cheaply than before. The specification cost is recovered whenever the work recurs, which is also the test for whether the delegation is worth proposing at all.

Distinguish it from the categories nearest to it. Coordination resolves who owns a boundary between parties who are already both involved, where delegation assigns a defined unit of work to one owner. Bottleneck diagnoses a limiting point in a system, and centralized execution is one cause of that; delegation is a possible repair rather than the diagnosis. Learning builds a capability the caller or the recipient lacks, which is the right suggestion when no capable recipient exists. Prerequisite creates a missing condition, and the absence of a qualified owner is exactly such a condition. Use delegation when the work is separable, a suitable owner exists, and the attention released exceeds the cost of handing it over.

## Use cases
- **The caller is the scarce decision-maker.** Routine execution may be crowding out choices only the caller can make. Move the defined work and keep the governing decisions.
- **Someone else has closer context.** A colleague, partner, or operator may be nearer to the users, the system, or the process. Suggest it when proximity improves execution and the expected result can be stated.
- **An agent can perform bounded preparation.** Research, formatting, inventory, comparison, or drafting may need no judgment from the caller. Use it when the agent works within clear authority and returns inspectable evidence.
- **Centralized execution is the constraint.** Too much work may be queued behind one person who does not need to perform every step. Recommend distribution when it relieves the constraint without creating coordination overload.
- **The work recurs often enough to specify.** A repeating activity may justify a standard handoff, checklist, and acceptance condition. Use it when the specification cost is recovered across repetitions.
- **Ownership would improve follow-through.** Work with no named executor lingers in the caller's mental queue. Assign a real owner with a commitment and an escalation path.
- **Review is adding oversight but not value.** Repeated checking often reflects implicit expectations rather than necessary judgment. Propose a better contract that reduces the review loop.
- **The work is a development opportunity.** A bounded responsibility may build someone's capability while advancing the task. Recommend it when the learning cost is acceptable and the risk boundary is explicit.
- **The caller is the wrong person on skill grounds.** Another owner may simply do the work better, faster, or more safely. Say so plainly, and define what the caller still decides.
- **Availability, not ability, is the constraint.** The caller may be capable and absent at the times the work is needed. Delegate for coverage, and define the authority that makes coverage real.
- **A task is blocking the caller's own critical path.** A small piece of unrelated work may be holding up something only the caller can advance. Move the small piece rather than reordering the critical work.
- **The work has an obvious natural owner nobody has named.** Responsibility may be clear to everyone and assigned to no one. Make the implicit assignment explicit, with its inputs and acceptance.

## When not to use
- **Judgment is required at every step.** Work that cannot be separated from the caller's decisions cannot be handed over.
- **No responsible recipient exists.** Delegating to nobody in particular is a way of losing work.
- **The handoff costs more than the task.** For small one-off work, explanation and review exceed execution.
- **Accountability would become ambiguous.** A transfer that blurs who answers for the outcome makes things worse than doing nothing.
- **The recipient lacks the capability.** Assigning work someone cannot do converts a task problem into a people problem.
- **Ownership is contested among several parties.** Assigning a unit of work does not settle a boundary dispute.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. A contested boundary belongs to coordination, and a missing capability belongs to learning or prerequisite. A limiting point in the system belongs to bottleneck, and competing claims on the caller's attention belong to prioritization. If the work is small and worth simply finishing, use completion or quick wins, and if it is not worth anyone's time, use stop or defer.
