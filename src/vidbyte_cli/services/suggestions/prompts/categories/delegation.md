# Delegation

## Description
A delegation suggestion moves a defined piece of work to the person or agent best placed to carry it. It distinguishes judgment that must remain with the caller from execution, preparation, or information gathering that can move elsewhere. The handoff must name the recipient, expected result, boundaries, authority, and feedback path. Delegation transfers execution responsibility without abandoning the caller’s accountability for the outcome. The caller should retain decisions that define quality, risk, or direction unless that authority is intentionally transferred. Use this category when the attention released is worth the cost of explaining and reviewing the work.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **The caller is the scarce decision-maker.** Routine execution is consuming attention needed for choices only the caller can make. Use delegation when another owner can complete the defined work while the caller retains the governing decisions.
- [ ] **Another person has closer context.** A team member, partner, or operator may be nearer to the users, system, or process involved. Suggest delegation when proximity improves execution and the expected result can be made explicit.
- [ ] **A capable agent can perform bounded preparation.** Research, formatting, inventory, comparison, or draft production may not require the caller’s judgment. Use the category when the agent can work within clear authority and return inspectable evidence.
- [ ] **A bottleneck is caused by centralized execution.** Too much work may be waiting for one person who need not perform every step. Recommend delegation when distributing the work reduces the constraint without creating coordination overload.
- [ ] **The work is repeatable enough to specify.** A recurring activity may be suitable for a standard handoff, checklist, or acceptance condition. Use it when the specification cost will be recovered through repeated delegation.
- [ ] **Ownership improves follow-through.** Work without a named executor can remain in the caller’s mental queue. Suggest this category when assigning a real owner creates a clear commitment and escalation path.
- [ ] **The caller’s involvement adds review but not value.** Some tasks are repeatedly checked because expectations are implicit rather than because judgment is needed at every step. Use delegation when a better contract can reduce unnecessary review loops.
- [ ] **A development opportunity matches the work.** Delegating a bounded responsibility may build another person’s capability while advancing the task. Recommend it when the learning cost is acceptable and the risk boundary is explicit.

## Things to consider
- What exact unit of work can be separated and handed off?
- Which decisions, authority, and risk must remain with the caller?
- Who has the capability, context, capacity, or incentive to own it?
- What result, inputs, boundaries, and deadline define a clean handoff?
- What access or information does the recipient need to start?
- How often should progress be checked without reclaiming execution?
- What acceptance evidence lets the caller review efficiently?
- Is explanation, coordination, and rework cost lower than doing the work directly?

## When not to use
Do not use this category when the work requires the caller’s unique judgment at every step or when no responsible recipient exists. Avoid it when the handoff would be more expensive than completing the small task or when accountability would become ambiguous. If the real issue is unclear ownership among several interdependent parties, use coordination; if the capability is missing, use learning or prerequisite.
