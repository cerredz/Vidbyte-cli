# Learning

## Description
A learning suggestion targets the knowledge or skill that will improve a specific upcoming task. It must be tied to an application, because learning detached from use is indistinguishable from an interesting detour and consumes the same time. The smallest useful lesson beats a broad curriculum almost always, since the curriculum defers the application that would have shown which parts mattered. What justifies the category is a capability gap rather than an information gap: the caller does not merely lack a fact, they cannot yet do the thing. Repeated difficulty with the same operation is the clearest evidence that the gap is durable and worth closing rather than working around. A single hard task is usually cheaper to get help with than to learn.

The suggestion should name the upcoming task or decision that will apply the learning, so the lesson has a deadline and a test. It should specify how understanding will be checked through practice, explanation, or a completed application, because reading and watching reliably produce confidence without capability. It should identify a source suited to the level of need rather than the most comprehensive one available. It should say what can be deferred until after the immediate commitment, since the instinct to learn the whole subject is what turns a two-hour gap into a two-week project. It should say what future action, judgment, or independence should improve, which is the only way to tell afterwards whether the investment paid. Where no application and no proof of understanding can be named, the suggestion is curiosity rather than learning.

## Why use
Use this category when the limiting factor is what the caller can do rather than what they know or have decided. That distinction matters because the repairs are different: a fact can be looked up in minutes, while a capability requires practice and a feedback loop. Treating a capability gap as an information gap produces a caller who has read about the thing and still cannot do it, which is a worse position than before because the gap is now hidden under the impression of preparation.

The category also makes a claim about economics that deserves to be explicit. Learning costs time now to reduce cost later, so it is justified by repetition, by the rising cost of dependence, or by the quality of judgment it enables. Where the work happens once and help is available, the honest answer is to get help. Where the same operation recurs, or where relying on others is producing delay and coordination burden, the investment repays and should be named as an investment with an expected return rather than as self-improvement.

Distinguish it from the categories around it. Investigation answers a question; learning builds the ability to answer that class of question unaided. Prerequisite creates a missing condition and is the right category when the capability is needed immediately and cannot be built in time, since someone else must then supply it. Delegation moves the work to someone who already has the capability, which is frequently the correct alternative to learning and should be compared honestly against it. Leverage captures a reusable organizational asset, where learning improves a person. Use learning when the caller will do this kind of work again and being able to do it themselves is worth the time.

## Use cases
- **The same capability gap recurs.** The caller may keep relearning or requesting help for one important operation. Build the skill when it will improve several upcoming attempts.
- **An upcoming decision needs domain understanding.** The caller may not know which evidence, trade, or mechanism matters here. Use a focused lesson when it would materially improve that decision.
- **A tool or method is blocking independence.** The caller may be unable to inspect, create, or change the work without assistance. Use it when a bounded practice path removes the dependency.
- **A mistake exposed a durable lesson.** The specific error may be fixed while the pattern behind it remains. Extract and practise the principle when that is worth more than the patch.
- **A new responsibility is arriving.** The caller may soon own a process, system, or conversation they have never handled. Build real competence rather than collecting material.
- **One concept would unlock a concrete task.** A single technique or workflow may be all that stands in the way. Choose the smallest lesson whose application is observable in the task.
- **Dependence is becoming expensive.** Relying on others may be producing delay and coordination burden. Internalize the capability when it improves judgment or execution enough to repay the cost.
- **A learning path needs proof of understanding.** Reading alone reliably produces false confidence. Require practice, explanation, or a completed application as the check.
- **The caller cannot evaluate work they are responsible for.** Accountability without competence turns review into rubber-stamping. Learn enough to judge, even if not enough to build.
- **A shared vocabulary is missing.** The caller may be unable to participate in decisions because the terms are opaque. Target the vocabulary needed for the conversation, not the field.
- **An assumption cannot be checked without the skill.** The plan may rest on a belief the caller has no way to evaluate. Learn the minimum required to test it.
- **A recurring workaround signals a missing fundamental.** Persistent improvisation often marks a concept nobody has learned. Name the concept and close it once.

## When not to use
- **The gap is a fact.** A question with a findable answer does not require building a capability.
- **Someone else must supply the condition.** A capability needed immediately and unavailable is a dependency, not a lesson.
- **Learning would only delay a safe action.** For cheap reversible work, doing it teaches faster than studying it.
- **No application can be named.** Without a task to apply it to, the lesson has no deadline and no test.
- **No proof of understanding is possible.** Learning that cannot be checked will be assumed rather than acquired.
- **The work happens once and help is available.** Building a skill for a single use rarely repays the time.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. A findable fact belongs to investigation, and a claim to confirm belongs to verification. A condition that must exist before work proceeds belongs to prerequisite, and moving the work to someone already capable belongs to delegation. A reusable organizational asset belongs to leverage, and a capability worth growing over an extended horizon belongs to long-term directions. If a cheap action would teach more than study would, use immediate next steps.
