# Quick Wins

## Description
A quick-win suggestion offers a small, inexpensive action with a useful near-term payoff. It can sit outside the main plan when its value is clear and its distraction cost is low. The benefit should be concrete rather than merely pleasant or easy to count. The action should not depend on a long chain of uncertain follow-up work. A quick win can build momentum, reduce friction, or create evidence for a larger choice. Use this category when its bounded nature makes the action worth taking soon without displacing a more important commitment.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **A small fix removes visible friction.** The caller may be losing time or clarity to a simple recurring annoyance. Use this category when the fix has a direct payoff and does not require a broad redesign.
- [ ] **A low-cost action creates immediate evidence.** A measurement, draft, request, or small comparison may clarify a larger choice. Suggest it when the evidence is useful even if no follow-up project occurs.
- [ ] **A simple default can improve consistency.** A reminder, template, setting, or documented choice may prevent repeated small errors. Use it when the default is safe and easy to reverse.
- [ ] **Momentum is valuable after a stalled period.** A modest completed action can restore engagement or make the work concrete. Recommend it only when completion produces progress, not just emotional relief.
- [ ] **A near-term benefit can fund or justify later work.** A small improvement may save time, reduce cost, or demonstrate demand. Use the category when the payoff can be observed within a short horizon.
- [ ] **An adjacent action is unusually cheap now.** Existing access, context, or setup may reduce the cost of a small improvement. Suggest a quick win when it remains truly bounded and does not become an adjacent project.
- [ ] **A reversible cleanup improves the current state.** Removing stale clutter, clarifying a label, or closing a minor loop may reduce future confusion. Use it when the cleanup has a specific user or workflow benefit.
- [ ] **A small change can test willingness or usability.** The caller may learn enough from a minimal exposure before investing further. Recommend it when the test has a meaningful signal and explicit stopping point.

## Things to consider
- What specific near-term payoff makes the action valuable?
- What time, attention, money, and coordination does it require?
- Which friction, uncertainty, or small obligation does it remove?
- What higher-priority work could it displace?
- Does the action create evidence or a durable improvement?
- Which dependencies and follow-up steps are truly absent or minimal?
- How soon and by what observation will the payoff appear?
- What is the stopping point if the benefit does not materialize?

## When not to use
Do not use this category when the action is required for the main goal, because continuation, completion, or prerequisite may describe it better. Avoid it when the benefit is only that the task is easy, or when follow-up work is large and uncertain. If the opportunity has major upside and meaningful exposure, use big bets; if attention is scarce, resolve the tradeoff with prioritization.
