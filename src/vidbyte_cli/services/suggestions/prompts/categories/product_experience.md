# Product and Experience

## Description
A product-and-experience suggestion improves what people can use and how that use feels or works. It begins with a user need, friction, or desired outcome rather than a feature label. The suggestion should identify the person and moment in which the improvement matters. It should balance usefulness, clarity, accessibility, trust, and the cost of added complexity. A strong experience idea explains how people would recognize the change as better in behavior or outcome. Use this category when a small representative interaction can test the improvement before broader product scope is committed.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **Users struggle at a specific moment.** A task, decision, or transition may contain friction that prevents people from reaching value. Use this category when the moment and affected users can be observed.
- [ ] **A feature exists but its value is unclear.** People may not discover, understand, or trust the available capability. Suggest a product-experience action when improving the interaction can change real use.
- [ ] **The current flow creates avoidable effort.** Extra steps, confusing language, or poor defaults may impede a meaningful outcome. Use it when the reduction can be tested without hiding necessary safeguards.
- [ ] **A product decision has an accessibility gap.** People with different abilities, devices, languages, or contexts may be excluded. Recommend the category when the affected experience and inclusive improvement can be specified.
- [ ] **A new capability needs a usable entry point.** Technical availability does not ensure that people can adopt or benefit from it. Use this category to connect capability design to a real user journey.
- [ ] **Trust or comprehension affects adoption.** People may hesitate because the system is opaque, surprising, or hard to recover from. Suggest an experience improvement when a clearer interaction can change that behavior.
- [ ] **A small prototype can answer an experience question.** The caller may not need a complete product build to learn what works. Use it when a representative interaction exposes the relevant behavior.
- [ ] **Different users experience competing tradeoffs.** A change may help one audience while adding burden or risk for another. Recommend this category when the experience can be evaluated across affected groups.

## Things to consider
- Which user, context, and moment contain the problem?
- What need, friction, or outcome is evidenced rather than assumed?
- What behavior would show that the experience improved?
- Which steps, language, defaults, or feedback create the current burden?
- What accessibility, trust, privacy, or recovery needs apply?
- What complexity or maintenance cost would the improvement add?
- Which users gain, lose, or remain unserved by the change?
- What smallest representative interaction can test the value?

## When not to use
Do not use this category for a technical capability with no identified user or system outcome, or for a business idea with no experience mechanism. Avoid it when the real obstacle is an unclear goal, missing evidence, or a required completion step. If the main decision is how the product should grow, use business and growth; if the work is about implementation feasibility, use technical possibilities.
