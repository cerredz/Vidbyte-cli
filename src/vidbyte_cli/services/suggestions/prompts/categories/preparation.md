# Preparation

## Purpose

Build readiness for an anticipated event, demand, or decision. Preparation reduces avoidable delay by making inputs, owners, materials, and fallback plans available before they are needed. It should be tied to a real trigger rather than general anxiety. The work is complete when the caller can meet the event's known requirements.

## Intent

Name the event, its expected trigger, the readiness gap, and the smallest preparation that closes that gap. Include a concrete check that distinguishes ready from merely busy.

## Timing

Use this category before a known launch, review, dependency, deadline, or demand creates pressure. Start early enough to preserve options, but do not prepare indefinitely for an event with no credible trigger.

## Checks

- State the event and the condition that makes preparation timely.
- Define the readiness check, owner, and fallback if the expected event arrives early.

## Cautions

- Do not use preparation as a substitute for an available execution step.
- Avoid producing materials that have no identified consumer or decision.
