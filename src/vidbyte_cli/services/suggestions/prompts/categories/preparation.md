# Preparation

## Description
A preparation suggestion creates readiness for an anticipated event, transition, or demand. It connects present work to a credible trigger and the response that will be needed when it arrives. Preparation may involve information, decisions, relationships, materials, access, or practice. The recommendation should finish before the trigger without relying on assumptions that will decay or become irrelevant. It should include a readiness signal that can be checked near the event. Use this category when a bounded investment can reduce expensive surprise without becoming an endless rehearsal.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **A known event has a hard response deadline.** A launch, meeting, migration, audit, or seasonal demand may leave no time to improvise. Use this category when readiness can be built and checked before the trigger.
- [ ] **A transition will change responsibilities or conditions.** New ownership, tooling, staffing, or policy may create predictable needs. Suggest preparation when the affected people and required readiness are identifiable.
- [ ] **A likely demand could overwhelm current capacity.** The caller may need materials, access, training, or fallback options before volume arrives. Use it when the demand signal is credible and preparation can be proportionate.
- [ ] **A critical decision must be made before the event.** Waiting until the trigger may remove safe or affordable choices. Recommend preparation when deciding early preserves options without overcommitting.
- [ ] **A relationship needs lead time.** Stakeholders, partners, reviewers, or suppliers may need advance notice to participate. Use this category when the outreach has a concrete purpose and timing.
- [ ] **Practice can reduce response error.** A rehearsal, simulation, or runbook may expose gaps before the real situation. Suggest it when the exercise tests the actual response and has a clear readiness threshold.
- [ ] **Inputs or materials take time to secure.** Access, inventory, data, equipment, or permissions may be unavailable at the last moment. Use preparation when securing them now reduces a known failure mode.
- [ ] **A fallback can protect the main response.** The primary plan may be sound but vulnerable to one predictable disruption. Recommend a proportionate contingency when its trigger and activation boundary are clear.

## Things to consider
- What event, transition, or demand triggers the need for readiness?
- How credible, consequential, and time-bound is the trigger?
- What must be ready in information, access, people, materials, decisions, or practice?
- Which readiness assumptions may decay before the event?
- What is the latest safe completion point?
- What observable test or signal proves readiness?
- What contingency is proportionate to the likely disruption?
- When should preparation stop so it does not become rehearsal without purpose?

## When not to use
Do not use this category for a missing condition required by work that cannot proceed now; use prerequisite instead. Avoid it when the event is too speculative to justify preparation or when no readiness signal can be defined. If the work is already underway and simply needs its next step, use continuation; if the concern is a broad future direction, use long-term directions.
