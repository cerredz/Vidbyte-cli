# Prioritization

## Description
A prioritization suggestion helps the caller choose among competing goals, tasks, or commitments. It makes the tradeoff explicit, including what will receive less attention as a result. The choice should consider consequence, urgency, reversibility, and the cost of delay without treating urgency as the only value. A useful priority reflects the caller’s stated outcomes and constraints rather than the loudest request. It should identify a small next commitment that makes the choice real. Use this category when allocation, not lack of ideas, is the primary decision.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **Several valid tasks compete for the same scarce attention.** Doing everything partially may produce less value than choosing one clearly. Use prioritization when the caller must allocate time, capacity, or decision bandwidth.
- [ ] **Delay has unequal consequences.** Some tasks lose value, create risk, or block others faster than the rest. Suggest a priority when comparing those costs can change the order.
- [ ] **The loudest request is not the most important.** Visibility, recency, or stakeholder pressure may distort allocation. Use this category to apply explicit values and consequences to the choice.
- [ ] **An irreversible option needs protection.** A decision or opportunity may become unavailable while reversible work can wait. Recommend prioritization when preserving that option should govern near-term attention.
- [ ] **A bottleneck requires focused capacity.** Several possible improvements may compete to relieve one constraint. Use prioritization when choosing the highest-impact intervention matters more than generating more interventions.
- [ ] **The caller needs to name displaced work.** A new commitment may be accepted without acknowledging what it displaces. Suggest this category when making the tradeoff explicit will prevent overload or hidden failure.
- [ ] **A deadline is approaching with incomplete scope.** The caller may need to choose the smallest valuable commitment under time pressure. Use prioritization when scope, quality, and timing can be traded consciously.
- [ ] **The order should be revisited after evidence arrives.** A temporary priority may be right now but not permanent. Recommend it when a review trigger or new information can change the allocation.

## Things to consider
- Which commitments compete for the same scarce resource?
- What outcome or value should govern the allocation?
- What is the consequence and cost of delay for each option?
- Which choices are reversible, and which opportunities expire?
- What dependency or bottleneck makes sequence matter?
- What work, quality, or relationship will be displaced?
- What smallest commitment makes the chosen priority operational?
- What evidence, date, or event should trigger reprioritization?

## When not to use
Do not use this category when there is only one meaningful commitment or when the next step is already determined by an accepted plan. Avoid it when the caller lacks a clear outcome or when the apparent competition is caused by a missing owner or prerequisite. If the work should be abandoned rather than ranked, use stop or defer; if a broader direction must be chosen, use strategy.
