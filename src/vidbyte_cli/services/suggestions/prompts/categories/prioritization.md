# Prioritization

## Description
A prioritization suggestion helps the caller choose among competing goals, tasks, or commitments that draw on the same scarce resource. Its defining obligation is to make the trade explicit, including what will receive less attention as a direct result of the choice. A ranking that does not name what falls below the line is not a prioritization; it is a wish list in order. The choice should weigh consequence, urgency, reversibility, and the cost of delay together, because urgency alone systematically favours whatever is loudest rather than whatever matters. It should reflect the caller's stated outcomes and constraints rather than the distribution of pressure around them. The output is useful only if it survives the next interruption.

The suggestion should identify the scarce resource precisely, since time, decision bandwidth, capital, and a specific person's attention are different constraints that produce different orderings. It should name what expires: opportunities that close, options that become irreversible, and work whose value decays with delay. It should account for dependency, because sequence sometimes matters more than value when one item unblocks several others. It should name the work, quality, or relationship being displaced, so that the cost of the choice is visible at the moment it is made rather than discovered later. It should propose the smallest commitment that makes the chosen priority operational, because a priority with no first action is a stated intention. It should also give the evidence, date, or event that should trigger reprioritization, since a priority correct today is not permanent.

## Why use
Use this category when the caller has no shortage of good options and cannot pursue them all. This is a specific failure mode with a specific cost: partial progress on everything, which in most systems delivers less value than complete progress on one thing, and which feels like diligence throughout. Naming the trade is what breaks it, and naming it is uncomfortable, which is precisely why it usually goes unsaid and why a suggestion that says it plainly is worth making.

The category also counteracts a reliable distortion. Attention flows towards recency, visibility, and social pressure rather than towards consequence, and it does so without anyone deciding that it should. Applying explicit values — what expires, what is irreversible, what blocks others, what the caller actually said they wanted — produces a different order from the one the environment generates by default. The gap between those two orders is the value of the suggestion.

Distinguish it from its neighbours. Strategy chooses a direction that coordinates many actions, which changes the criteria rather than applying them to a fixed list. Stop or defer concludes that specific work should leave the list entirely, which prioritization assumes it does not. Goal clarification supplies the outcome that prioritization ranks against, so unclear goals must be settled first or the ranking will be arbitrary. Bottleneck explains why progress is slow for structural reasons rather than because of how attention is allocated. Use prioritization when several worthwhile commitments compete, the outcome is clear, and allocation is the decision to be made.

## Use cases
- **Several valid tasks compete for the same attention.** Partial progress on all of them may deliver less than completing one. Allocate the scarce time, capacity, or decision bandwidth explicitly.
- **Delay costs differ sharply.** Some items lose value, create risk, or block others faster than the rest. Compare those costs when doing so changes the order.
- **The loudest request is not the most important.** Visibility, recency, or pressure may be distorting allocation. Apply stated values and consequences instead.
- **An irreversible option needs protecting.** A decision or opportunity may expire while reversible work waits safely. Let preservation govern near-term attention.
- **Focused capacity must go to one constraint.** Several improvements may compete to relieve the same limiter. Choose the highest-impact intervention rather than generating more of them.
- **Displaced work needs naming.** A new commitment may be accepted without acknowledging what it pushes out. Make the trade explicit to prevent overload and hidden failure.
- **A deadline is approaching with incomplete scope.** Scope, quality, and timing may need to be traded consciously. Choose the smallest valuable commitment under the constraint.
- **The order should be provisional.** Today's priority may be right and temporary. Name the review trigger that would change it.
- **Everything has been marked important.** Uniform priority is the absence of prioritization. Force a ranking, and say what the bottom of it means.
- **One person is the constraint across several items.** The scarce resource may be a specific individual rather than time in general. Order against that person's availability, not the team's.
- **Sequence matters more than value.** One lower-value item may unblock several higher-value ones. Order by dependency when the graph dominates the scores.
- **New work keeps arriving mid-commitment.** Continuous intake may be quietly displacing whatever was agreed. Establish what interrupts and what waits.

## When not to use
- **Only one meaningful commitment exists.** A ranking of one is not a decision.
- **An accepted plan already determines the step.** Re-ranking settled work reopens it for no gain.
- **The outcome is unclear.** Ranking against an undefined goal produces a confident arbitrary order.
- **The competition comes from a missing owner or condition.** Apparent contention sometimes dissolves once the real blocker is named.
- **The work should be abandoned rather than ranked.** Keeping dead work on a list keeps it in mind.
- **A broader direction has to be chosen.** Criteria that do not exist yet cannot be applied.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. An undefined outcome belongs to goal clarification, and a direction that sets the criteria belongs to strategy. Work that should leave the list belongs to stop or defer, and a missing condition or owner belongs to prerequisite or coordination. A structural limit on progress belongs to bottleneck, and an accepted plan's next move belongs to continuation. If the caller has capacity but cannot start anything, use immediate next steps rather than another ranking.
