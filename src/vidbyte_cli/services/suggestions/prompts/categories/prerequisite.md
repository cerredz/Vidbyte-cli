# Prerequisite

## Description
A prerequisite is a condition that intended work cannot safely or effectively proceed without. The condition may be evidence, access, capability, authority, preparation, or a prior result, and what unites those forms is that the work stops until the condition exists. The suggestion's job is to make a dependency explicit that has so far been an unexamined source of delay. Its usefulness rests on naming the specific work that becomes possible once the condition is met, because a requirement with no unlocked work behind it is not a requirement anyone needs to act on. The category demands a hard distinction between a true requirement and a strong preference, since those are treated identically by whoever is blocked and are not the same thing at all. Drawing that line is frequently the whole of the contribution.

The suggestion should name who can provide, approve, or create the condition, since an unowned dependency stays unmet however clearly it is described. It should state the observable evidence that the condition exists and remains valid, because conditions expire quietly — credentials lapse, approvals go stale, agreed facts change. It should identify the cheapest safe way to obtain the condition rather than the most complete one. It should ask whether a narrower route or a reduced scope could proceed without it, because a great deal of blocked work is blocked by an ideal setup rather than a genuine gate. It should say what happens if the condition is still unavailable at the decision point, so the blockage has an ending. A prerequisite without an escalation path becomes a permanent excuse.

## Why use
Use this category when work is not moving and the reason is a condition nobody has named. This is different from a hard problem and different from competing priorities, and it is often invisible to the people closest to the work, who experience it as things being slow. Naming the condition converts an ambient delay into a specific, ownable, obtainable item. That conversion is cheap and is frequently the difference between a week of drift and an afternoon of unblocking.

The category earns its place equally often by making the opposite move. Callers block themselves on conditions that are preferences: a cleaner environment, a complete dataset, a decision that could be made later, an approval nobody actually requires. Testing the condition against what the work genuinely needs releases work that was never truly blocked. That is why the suggestion must ask whether a reduced scope could proceed — the answer is yes more often than the person inside the situation expects.

Distinguish it from its neighbours. Preparation builds readiness for a future event, where a prerequisite blocks work that is wanted now; the tense is the difference. Bottleneck describes a point that limits flow through a system over time, whereas a prerequisite is a specific condition for a specific piece of work. Coordination aligns parties around an interface, and while an agreement can be a prerequisite, the category applies whether or not other parties are involved. Learning builds a capability over time, and prerequisite is the right framing when the capability is needed now and must come from elsewhere. Use prerequisite when one nameable condition stands between the caller and work they are otherwise ready to do.

## Use cases
- **The next task lacks access or authority.** Work may be ready in every other respect and unable to start legally, technically, or organizationally. Identify the exact approval, credential, or permission.
- **A decision needs a missing fact or result.** Proceeding without it may invalidate everything built on top. Use it when obtaining the input is necessary rather than merely useful.
- **A capability must exist before execution.** A skill, tool, capacity, or process may be required for safe completion. Use it when the gap blocks a defined piece of work.
- **Setup is required for safe progress.** A backup, test environment, or advance communication may be a condition of proceeding. Recommend it when the risk attaches directly to the intended action.
- **A dependency is hidden in the plan.** The work may look sequentially ready while one external condition stays unstated. Expose the dependency and name its owner.
- **A preference is masquerading as a requirement.** An ideal setup may be delaying useful action. Test the condition, and say what could proceed without it.
- **An earlier result gates a later phase.** A measurement, review, prototype, or agreement may determine whether the next phase should begin. Use it when the gate is explicit and meaningful.
- **The condition has more than one route.** A missing condition may be obtainable several ways or bypassed at reduced scope. Compare the routes rather than accepting the blockage.
- **A licence, contract, or compliance step is outstanding.** Formal conditions often sit unnoticed until they stop a release. Surface them early, with the owner and the lead time.
- **A shared environment or dataset is unavailable.** Work may be waiting on something that has to be built, restored, or granted. Name what it is and who supplies it.
- **An upstream deliverable has not arrived.** Another party may owe an artifact the work depends on. State what is owed, by whom, and by when.
- **The condition has expired without anyone noticing.** An approval, key, or agreement may have lapsed while the work proceeded. Check validity rather than existence.

## When not to use
- **The information is interesting but not blocking.** A curiosity does not become a requirement by being relevant.
- **The condition already exists.** Re-obtaining what is available wastes the unblocking effort.
- **The real issue is a queue, an owner, or a decision.** Those are different failures with different repairs.
- **Readiness is for a future event.** A condition for later work is preparation, not a present gate.
- **The claim that it is required has not been tested.** Unexamined requirements are how work blocks itself.
- **No one can supply it and no alternative was considered.** A dependency with no route is an abandonment dressed as a plan.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. Future readiness belongs to preparation, and a limiting point in the flow belongs to bottleneck. A missing owner or misaligned interface belongs to coordination, and competing claims on capacity belong to prioritization. A claim to be checked belongs to verification, and a fact to be found belongs to investigation. If the capability gap is durable and the caller will need it repeatedly, use learning, and if no route to the condition exists at all, use alternative or stop or defer.
