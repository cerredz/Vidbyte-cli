# Creative Exploration

## Description
A creative-exploration suggestion deliberately widens the space of possibilities beyond the assumptions currently in play. It works by varying one input — audience, medium, constraint, sequence, scale, or definition of value — and reading off the options that variation makes visible. The output is a concrete set of meaningfully distinct possibilities, not an invitation to brainstorm, because an invitation produces more of whatever the group already thinks. Distinctness is the property that matters: options that differ in mechanism or in who benefits change a decision, and options that differ in wording do not. Novelty on its own is worthless here and is the main way the category gets wasted. What justifies the exercise is contrast that a later choice can act on.

The exploration must be bounded or it becomes a detour with no return path. The suggestion should say how many options are enough to create contrast without generating noise, what time or attention budget contains the exercise, and what rule keeps familiar variants from filling the slate. It should name the criterion or signal that will identify a promising direction, because generating options without a selection rule simply relocates the difficulty. It should say how a selected idea comes back to the original goal or to a concrete test, since an unconnected idea is entertainment. It should also name the assumption being varied explicitly, so that the exercise can be repeated with a different one if the first pass yields nothing. Exploration that cannot state what it varied has not explored anything.

## Why use
Use this category when the option set is too narrow and everyone has stopped noticing. Groups converge, and convergence looks like agreement long after it has become a blind spot; the proposals all differ in detail and share a premise nobody has examined. In that state, evaluating the existing options more carefully cannot help, because the best available option may not be in the set. Widening the set first is the only move that changes the outcome, and it has to be done deliberately, since the natural drift is towards refinement.

The category also buys insurance against premature narrowing. Early commitment is efficient when the field is well understood and expensive when it is not, and the cost of discarding an option before its value was visible is invisible by construction. A short, bounded exploration is a cheap hedge against that cost. Bounding it is what makes the hedge affordable, and it is why this category insists on a budget and a selection rule rather than treating open-endedness as a virtue.

Keep the boundary against its neighbours clear. Alternative compares a small number of routes against a fixed outcome and is the right category once the aim is to choose rather than to widen. Cross-domain borrows a specific mechanism with evidence behind it from another field, which is a targeted transfer rather than a general expansion. Reframing changes the interpretation of the problem itself, producing one new reading rather than a slate of options. Big bets and long-term directions concern commitment and capability over time, not the generation of possibilities. Use creative exploration when the useful next act is to see more of the field.

## Use cases
- **Every current option looks alike.** Repeated proposals may share an assumption about audience, mechanism, or acceptable scope. Vary the assumption and produce options that could genuinely change the decision.
- **A familiar solution pattern is exhausted.** Optimizing a route that no longer yields gains is a sign the route is the limit. Change the frame or the medium to expose a different mechanism.
- **The beneficiary is defined too narrowly.** A different user, stakeholder, or setting may reveal an unmet need or a different value exchange. Use it when broadening the perspective yields testable options.
- **Constraints are stopping ideas rather than shaping them.** A budget, deadline, policy, or resource limit may be treated as a reason not to think. Run a constrained exercise that turns the limit into a generator of distinct possibilities.
- **Several futures need prepared responses.** Conditions may be unknown while the plausible responses are enumerable. Use the category when the exercise ends in a small option set tied to recognizable signals.
- **A group is trapped in one vocabulary.** People may be unable to see options because everyone describes the problem the same way. Suggest a concrete reframing, analogy, or role shift that changes the available language.
- **A little novelty would sharpen a test.** One unusual concept may point at a better experiment or interaction. Use it when a path back to the original goal exists and a selection rule is defined.
- **Narrowing early is riskier than exploring.** A short session may preserve meaningful choice at negligible cost. Recommend it when the decision is not yet due and the field is poorly mapped.
- **The same objection kills every proposal.** A recurring blocker may be a constraint worth designing against directly. Generate options that attack the constraint rather than avoiding it.
- **The work has an unexamined default.** A format, channel, scale, or sequence may have been inherited rather than chosen. Vary the default explicitly and see what appears.
- **A scarce resource is assumed fixed.** Time, attention, budget, or access may be treated as immovable when it is merely conventional. Explore options that change the resource rather than working within it.
- **The problem is being solved at one scale only.** A much smaller or much larger version may have a different and better answer. Generate options at both extremes before returning to the middle.

## When not to use
- **A clear route already exists.** Widening the field when the choice is made wastes the decision's remaining time.
- **No selection rule or boundary exists.** Options with no way to choose among them are noise with extra steps.
- **The request is for more ideas with no decision attached.** Generation without a destination has no stopping condition.
- **The novelty needed is a proven mechanism from elsewhere.** Borrowing a specific working pattern is a targeted act, not an expansion.
- **Only one interpretation needs to change.** A single new reading of the problem is a different move from a slate of options.
- **The decision is due now.** Exploration that arrives after the choice is made costs attention and returns nothing.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. Execution belongs to continuation, and choosing among competing commitments belongs to prioritization. Comparing a few routes to a fixed outcome is alternative, and borrowing a tested mechanism from another field is cross-domain. Changing the interpretation of the problem is reframing, and testing whether one promising idea holds is experiment. If the exploration is stalling because nobody agrees what a good outcome looks like, use goal clarification first.
