# Big Bets

## Description
A big bet is an ambitious move with unusually high potential upside and meaningful uncertainty. Its scale, irreversibility, or exposure can overwhelm ordinary next-step advice. The suggestion must make the hoped-for gain concrete instead of relying on excitement or status. It must name the capital, capability, timing, and failure exposure required to pursue the move. A staged commitment can create learning before the full bet is placed, but it cannot erase the underlying uncertainty. Use this category only when the opportunity is important enough to justify deliberate risk and concentrated attention.

## Why use / use cases
- [ ] **The upside could change the trajectory.** The opportunity may create a new level of reach, capability, revenue, or impact rather than a marginal improvement. Use this category when that upside is plausible enough to investigate and material enough to matter.
- [ ] **The current ceiling is strategically limiting.** Incremental improvements may not address a constraint that prevents the desired scale or position. Suggest a big bet when a step-change is needed and the organization can discuss the associated exposure honestly.
- [ ] **A rare window favors decisive action.** Technology shifts, market timing, policy changes, or unusual access may make a large move more valuable now. Use it when the window is evidenced and the cost of waiting can be articulated.
- [ ] **A capability requires concentrated investment.** Some outcomes need a dedicated team, platform, partnership, or long build before benefits appear. Suggest the category when spreading small efforts would fail to create the required capability.
- [ ] **A bold commitment can unlock complementary assets.** A visible decision may attract talent, partners, distribution, or credibility that small experiments cannot. Use it only when the enabling effect is more than a hopeful narrative.
- [ ] **The downside is survivable and bounded.** A large risk may be acceptable when failure does not threaten the core obligation or the ability to recover. The suggestion should specify the loss limit and the conditions that preserve survivability.
- [ ] **The decision deserves explicit executive attention.** A bet may cross a threshold where ordinary task prioritization would conceal its strategic consequences. Use this category to surface the decision and its evidence, not to bypass governance.
- [ ] **A staged path can reduce uncertainty before commitment.** Discovery, a paid pilot, or a capability milestone may test the most important assumption first. Recommend the category when a staged path still preserves a meaningful route to the full upside.

## Things to consider
- What measurable upside makes this a bet rather than an incremental improvement?
- Which assumptions control the size and probability of the payoff?
- What capital, capability, attention, and time must be committed?
- Which part of the commitment is reversible, and which part is not?
- What is the maximum acceptable loss to the core work or organization?
- What staged evidence would justify increasing the commitment?
- Which external conditions make the timing unusually favorable or dangerous?
- Who has authority to place the bet and who bears the downside?

## When not to use
Do not use this category for ordinary improvements, speculative ideas with no material upside, or proposals whose cost has not been bounded. Avoid it when a small experiment can answer the key question before any large commitment. If the issue is selecting a broad direction among several viable paths, use strategy; if it is preventing a specific failure, use risk prevention.
