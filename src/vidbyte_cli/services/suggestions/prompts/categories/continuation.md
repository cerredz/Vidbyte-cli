# Continuation

## Description
A continuation suggestion advances an already accepted plan from its current state to its next bounded step. It assumes the direction is settled and the desired outcome is clear enough to act on, which is what makes it the cheapest useful suggestion available when those assumptions hold. Its job is to identify what happens next without reopening anything that was already decided. The step must be concrete enough to start without further planning and narrow enough that finishing it produces a natural point to stop and reassess. A continuation is not a new strategy wearing execution language, and it is not a request to repeat effort that produced no learning. Where the direction is genuinely in doubt, the honest suggestion is something other than continuation.

The suggestion should name what has already been accepted, because a continuation that misidentifies the plan will carry work in the wrong direction efficiently. It should say what changed in the current state since the previous step, since that change is usually what makes the next action visible. It should supply the input, the owner, and the output that make the action startable, because unowned obvious steps are the ones that stall longest. It should give a bounded result that marks the step complete, so that momentum does not become drift. It should check whether executing the step would create an irreversible commitment ahead of a decision that has not been made yet. It should also name the condition under which reassessment replaces another continuation, because a plan that can never be interrupted is not a plan.

## Why use
Use this category when the situation needs movement rather than thought. A surprising amount of stalled work is not blocked by uncertainty at all; it is blocked because nobody has named the next action in terms concrete enough to begin. Naming it is a small act with a disproportionate effect, and it is the correct recommendation far more often than the more interesting categories are. A suggestion agent that reaches for strategy when continuation would do produces work that feels insightful and changes nothing.

The category also protects a plan from being reopened for no reason. Every re-examination has a cost — context reload, renegotiation, lost attention — and plans that are re-examined continuously never reach the point where their assumptions could be tested against reality. Continuation asserts that the prior decision stands and that the useful next act is to execute against it. That assertion should be made deliberately, which is why the suggestion must state what was accepted and what would trigger reassessment instead.

Keep the boundary against its neighbours firm. Completion applies when the step being proposed is the last one and closing it ends the obligation. Immediate next steps starts from the current state without assuming an accepted plan, which makes it the right category when the direction is only loosely agreed. Prerequisite applies when a condition required by the plan does not yet exist, and proposing the next step over a missing condition simply relocates the blockage. Alternative and strategy both reopen the route, which is the opposite of what this category does. Use continuation when the plan holds, the state is known, and one bounded action moves it forward.

## Use cases
- **An accepted plan has paused.** The direction is settled and the current state is known. Name the action that restores movement without reopening the plan.
- **A milestone has just been reached.** Completing one step may have made the next dependency visible. Suggest continuation when the new state supports a bounded follow-on rather than a fresh planning cycle.
- **The next step is obvious but unowned.** Work stalls even when no strategic question remains. Supply the action, owner, input, and completion signal that carry the plan forward.
- **A sequence has a natural next operation.** One stage may produce exactly what the following stage consumes. Recommend it when the dependency is explicit and the handoff is ready.
- **Restart cost is rising.** A long pause may require reloading context, rebuilding setup, or recapturing stakeholder attention. Use it when starting now has a real continuity benefit rather than an imagined one.
- **The next action can produce value independently.** A plan may continue while a later decision stays open. Suggest it when the step creates no irreversible commitment ahead of that decision.
- **An accepted task is still too broad to begin.** The plan may be clear while the immediate action is not. Reduce it to one executable slice with a stopping condition.
- **A predictable follow-up is part of the agreed path.** A review, measurement, deployment, or communication may already be an obligation of the plan. Recommend it as continuation rather than presenting it as a new idea.
- **A parallel track can start now.** An independent piece of the accepted plan may be startable while the main line waits. Use it when the parallel work needs nothing the blocked line is waiting for.
- **A step failed and the plan survives.** An attempt may have produced a correctable failure without invalidating the direction. Suggest the corrected next attempt when the learning is specific and the plan still holds.
- **New input has arrived that the plan expected.** Data, a decision, or an approval may have unblocked a step that was already scheduled. Name the step the arrival enables.
- **Work is proceeding without a defined stopping point.** An in-progress effort may need a bounded target more than it needs a new one. Propose the boundary as the continuation itself.

## When not to use
- **The direction is disputed.** Executing against a contested plan converts disagreement into wasted work.
- **The goal is unclear.** A next step towards an undefined outcome cannot be evaluated.
- **A required condition is missing.** Advancing over an absent prerequisite moves the blockage rather than removing it.
- **The proposed step is really a new idea.** An opportunity, a different route, or a broader direction should be argued as one.
- **This step is the last remaining obligation.** Closing work has different evidence requirements from advancing it.
- **Continuing is no longer worthwhile.** Momentum is not a reason to keep spending when the value has gone.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. A missing condition belongs to prerequisite, and a final obligation belongs to completion. A disputed route belongs to alternative or strategy, and an undefined outcome belongs to goal clarification. Work that no longer earns its cost belongs to stop or defer, and a plan that does not exist yet is better served by immediate next steps. If the step cannot start because one point in the system is holding everything up, use bottleneck instead.
