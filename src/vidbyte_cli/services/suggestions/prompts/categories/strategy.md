# Strategy

## Description
A strategy suggestion chooses a broader direction that coordinates several later actions. Its purpose is to make trades, opportunity costs, and boundaries visible before local execution compounds them into a shape nobody chose. A direction must constrain: if it permits every action that was already permitted, it is a description rather than a strategy, and it will guide nothing. At the same time it must not pretend to knowledge it lacks, so it constrains at the level of principle and leaves the details to be discovered. It connects its rationale to the caller's stated outcome, context, and evidence, since a direction detached from those is a preference with authority borrowed from the word. The test is whether two people applying it to the same decision would reach the same answer.

The suggestion should state the non-goals as explicitly as the goals, because what will not be optimized is where a strategy does its work and is the part most commonly omitted. It should compare the alternatives that were rejected and say what each would have cost, so the choice can be examined rather than accepted. It should name the assumptions that connect the direction to the desired outcome, since those are what a changing environment will attack first. It should identify leading signals that show progress before final results arrive, and the evidence that would require revision. It should name a review point, because an unreviewable direction becomes an identity. It should also propose one immediate action that tests whether the strategy is usable in practice, as directions that cannot be applied to a real decision this week usually cannot be applied at all.

## Why use
Use this category when local decisions keep conflicting because nothing connects them. That pattern is recognizable: each choice is defensible, the aggregate is incoherent, and no individual decision can be identified as the mistake. The missing input is a direction, and adding one resolves not just the current decision but the class of decisions it belongs to. That multiplier is what makes strategy worth the effort it costs, and it is also why strategy applied to a single decision is overhead.

The category also exists to force a trade into the open. Organizations avoid stating what they will not do, because non-goals create losers immediately while goals create winners eventually. The result is a direction that everyone can agree with and nobody can apply, which is worse than no direction because it consumes the occasion for making a real one. Requiring explicit non-goals and rejected alternatives is what keeps this category from producing that.

Distinguish it from its neighbours carefully. Alternative chooses between two concrete routes to one outcome, where strategy sets the criteria by which such choices get made. Big bets asks for a large, concentrated, partly irreversible commitment, which a direction may imply but does not require. Long-term directions concerns capability worth developing over a horizon without demanding present subordination. Prioritization ranks commitments against criteria that already exist, and where the criteria are the problem, ranking harder will not help. Goal clarification defines the outcome the strategy serves and must come first when it is missing. Use strategy when one directional choice would make several later decisions coherent.

## Use cases
- **Several actions need a shared direction.** Local decisions may conflict because no principle connects them. Choose the direction that makes later sequencing and trades coherent.
- **Concentration must be decided.** Time, capital, capability, or attention cannot support every plausible route. Set the allocation when it should shape multiple downstream commitments.
- **A recurring decision needs a governing principle.** Teams may be choosing inconsistently under similar conditions. Define the direction that makes those choices faster and aligned.
- **The environment has changed materially.** New constraints, opportunities, competitors, users, or technology may have invalidated the old path. Use it when the response must coordinate more than one adjustment.
- **Several viable paths have different consequences.** The choice may affect reach, quality, speed, resilience, or future options. Compare at the level of direction rather than task by task.
- **A position must be built deliberately.** Short-term actions may not accumulate into durable advantage without consistency. Use it when sequencing over time is what creates the value.
- **An explicit trade is needed.** A direction can state what will not be pursued or optimized. Use it when the avoided option matters as much as the chosen one.
- **The direction should stay conditional.** Evidence may still be accumulating while decisions cannot wait. Name the leading indicators and the review point that keep it accountable.
- **Two parts of the organization are pulling apart.** Divergence usually reflects an unstated directional choice rather than bad faith. Make the choice, and say which local goal yields.
- **Repeated escalations land on the same question.** A decision that keeps returning to one person is a missing principle. Convert the judgment into a rule others can apply.
- **A default is operating unexamined.** An inherited direction may still be shaping decisions long after its rationale expired. Restate or replace it deliberately.
- **Scope keeps growing because nothing is excluded.** Without non-goals, every reasonable request qualifies. Draw the boundary at the directional level rather than per request.

## When not to use
- **The next action is already clear.** A direction added on top of a settled action is ceremony.
- **One narrow check would resolve it.** Strategy is an expensive answer to a factual question.
- **The direction has no trade.** A statement everyone endorses constrains nobody.
- **It cannot guide more than one decision.** A direction for a single choice is just that choice.
- **The outcome itself is undefined.** A direction towards nothing in particular cannot be evaluated.
- **The commitment is unusually large and uncertain.** Exposure of that size deserves to be named rather than implied.

Route the suggestion elsewhere when one of those signals holds. Two concrete routes to one outcome belong to alternative, and a large uncertain commitment belongs to big bets. A capability worth developing over a horizon belongs to long-term directions, and ranking against existing criteria belongs to prioritization. An undefined outcome belongs to goal clarification, and a factual question belongs to investigation or verification. If the direction is clear and the problem is that nothing is moving, use continuation or immediate next steps.
