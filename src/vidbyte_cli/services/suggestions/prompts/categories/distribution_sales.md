# Distribution and Sales

## Description

A distribution-and-sales suggestion improves how an offering reaches potential customers and turns
interest into a purchase. It includes channels, sales motions, referrals, partnerships, commerce
flows, and ways to reduce the effort or uncertainty of buying. The category starts with where people
already look for solutions and how much explanation or trust the decision requires. A useful proposal
matches channel economics and sales effort to the value and complexity of the offer. It distinguishes
attention from qualified demand and a qualified lead from a completed purchase. It should define a
bounded channel or sales experiment with a clear owner, cost, and decision rule.

## Why use / use cases

- **Potential customers do not encounter the offer.** The product may be useful but absent from the
  places the audience searches. Use this category when a reachable discovery path can be named.
- **Interest fails to become a conversation or trial.** The handoff between marketing and sales may
  lose people. Use it when the missing step and its evidence can be isolated.
- **A high-trust purchase needs explanation.** Buyers may require proof, consultation, or security
  review before committing. Use the lens when the sales motion can reduce that uncertainty.
- **The buying flow creates avoidable friction.** Confusing qualification, checkout, or procurement
  steps can suppress conversion. Use it when the friction can be removed without removing safeguards.
- **A referral or partner channel may fit.** Another trusted party may already have access to the
  audience. Use the category when incentives, ownership, and economics are explicit.
- **A channel appears promising but is expensive.** Volume can conceal poor conversion or payback.
  Use this lens to test qualified demand and full acquisition cost.
- **Self-serve and sales-assisted routes need separation.** Different segments may need different
  levels of help. Use it when routing rules can improve both access and sales efficiency.
- **A new offer needs a repeatable launch route.** One successful sale is not a distribution system.
  Use this category when repeatability and capacity can be measured.

## Things to consider

- Where does the target customer already search, compare, or ask for help?
- How much trust, education, or proof does the purchase require?
- Which step separates awareness, qualified interest, trial, and purchase?
- What channel cost, conversion, payback, and capacity constraints apply?
- Who owns the lead, handoff, follow-up, and customer expectation?
- What partner or referral incentive could create durable access rather than one-off volume?
- Which customers should be self-serve, sales-assisted, or partner-served?
- What smallest channel test has a pre-set continue, change, or stop decision?

## When not to use

Do not use this category when the main question is whether a market wants the problem solved, what
the offer should mean, or how customers are supported after purchase. Avoid channel ideas that count
reach without a path to qualified demand or value. Use business model and monetization when the core
change is price or packaging, and brand and positioning when the core change is the remembered promise.
